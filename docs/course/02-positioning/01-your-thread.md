# Lesson 2.1: Your thread

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/02-positioning/01-your-thread.md)

**Time:** 40 minutes

## Goal

Find the recurring thread that makes your videos feel like a series and gives a viewer a reason to follow you, even though your videos are about *you* and not a narrow topic.

## "Niche" for a personal channel

Advice about "picking a niche" usually assumes you are teaching one subject: baking, fitness, finance. If you are making reels about your life, work and ideas, a narrow niche can feel like a trap. The better question is: **what is the thread that connects my videos?**

A thread has three parts:

1. **A point of view.** How you see something. "Engineering thinking applied to everyday life."
2. **A journey or tension.** Something in motion. "Switching careers", "building something on the side", "learning a language as an adult".
3. **A promise to the viewer.** What a person gets from following you. "A smart, honest look at how a career change actually feels."

If you cannot answer "why would a stranger watch the second video after the first one?", you do not yet have a thread.

## Why a thread matters

* **It lets viewers sort themselves.** The right people recognise "this is for me" quickly.
* **It makes ideas easier.** A thread is a filter. If an idea does not fit, you skip it.
* **It builds a loyal audience.** In Kevin Kelly's essay [1000 True Fans](https://kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans/), a true fan is someone who will buy whatever you make. A thread gives people a reason to become one.

A thread is not a prison. You can change it. But for your first 10 reels, pick one, so you get a clear signal.

## How to find yours

Use three lists. Spend ten minutes on each, writing fast.

**List A: What I know that others find interesting.** Skills, past jobs, places, unusual experience. The unusual combination is often the most interesting. Example: a person who moved from civil engineering to software has a view on both worlds.

**List B: What I'm going through right now.** Projects, decisions, struggles, learnings. Reels about work in progress are easy to make because the material is fresh and honest.

**List C: What I keep explaining to friends.** The things people ask you about. If you tell the same story or give the same advice repeatedly, it already works as content.

Now look for overlaps. Circle the items that appear in two lists. These are your candidates.

## Turn candidates into a thread statement

Fill in this sentence for two or three candidates:

> *I make short videos about [point of view] for people who [situation or desire], so they can [benefit].*

Examples:

* "I make short videos about what changing careers in your thirties is really like, for people who are thinking about it, so they can see the honest version."
* "I make short videos about building small software tools, for people who want to start side projects, so they can see how it works without the hype."

Test each statement:

* Can I make 20 different videos inside it? (If not, it is too narrow.)
* Can I explain it to a friend in one breath? (If not, it is too vague.)
* Does it feel true and sustainable, something I will not be bored of in a month?

## Choose one and commit to a test

Pick the statement you feel most energy about. Commit to it for the **first 10 reels**. After that, your analytics (lesson 7.2) will show which angles hold attention, and you can adjust.

## Example

Someone with a background in civil engineering who moved to programming might choose: *"Engineer's brain, real life: how technical thinking changes ordinary decisions."* Videos fit naturally: how to plan a trip like a project, what mistakes teach you about systems, why checklists matter. All are personal, all share a viewpoint, none require expertise on a single topic.

## Common mistakes

* **Choosing what you think will be popular.** Boredom ends streaks. Pick something you can talk about for months.
* **Making it too broad.** "Life" is not a thread.
* **Making it too narrow.** "Only Python decorators" can limit you before you start.
* **Waiting for certainty.** You will not know until you have filmed ten reels.

## Exercise

1. Write the three lists.
2. Circle overlaps.
3. Write two thread statements.
4. Write 10 video ideas for the one you pick. If you cannot, pick the other.
5. Write the chosen thread on a note and put it where you film.

## Daily practice

* **Minimum (keeps the streak):** a voice note of any length.
* **Today's take (about 30 seconds):** Say your thread statement to the lens in one breath, three times. Keep the third.
* Weeks 1-2 everything stays private; from week 3 follow your publishing plan.
* Don't watch the take right away (lesson 4.1). Tick the day in your tracker.

## Checklist

* [ ] I have a thread statement I can say in one breath.
* [ ] I have 10 ideas that fit it.
* [ ] I committed to it for the first 10 reels.
* [ ] I understand it can change after I see data.
* [ ] I recorded today's voice note and take.

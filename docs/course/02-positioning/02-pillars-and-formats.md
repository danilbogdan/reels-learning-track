# Lesson 2.2: Pillars and formats

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/02-positioning/02-pillars-and-formats.md)

**Time:** 40 minutes

## Goal

Turn your thread into an ongoing supply of ideas by defining 3-4 content pillars and a small set of repeatable formats.

## The problem this solves

A common failure point is not filming; it is deciding *what* to film. If every video starts from zero, you spend your energy on choosing and run out before you press record. Pillars and formats turn "what do I make today?" into a quick pick from a menu.

## Content pillars

A pillar is a recurring area inside your thread. Three or four is enough. Each pillar answers a different reason someone follows you.

Example thread: *Honest look at changing careers and building side projects.*

* **Pillar 1: The journey** (what is happening now). Updates, decisions, setbacks.
* **Pillar 2: Lessons** (what I learned). Mistakes, mental models, tips.
* **Pillar 3: How things work** (behind the scenes). Tools, routines, systems.
* **Pillar 4: Opinions** (what I think). Takes on common advice.

Notice each pillar can generate dozens of videos, and each has a different feel: updates are casual, lessons are structured, how-things-work is visual, opinions are punchy. Variety inside a thread is healthy.

## Formats

A format is a repeatable shape for a video. It has a fixed skeleton and a variable content. Formats are what make a series feel like a series, and they make production faster because the editing, framing and caption style repeat.

Ten formats to start from:

1. **Talking head with one idea.** You, close to the camera, one point, 20-30 seconds.
2. **Three things.** "Three mistakes / habits / tools." Three beats of five seconds each.
3. **Story in the middle.** Start at the dramatic moment, explain how you got there, finish with the lesson.
4. **Before and after.** A situation, then what changed.
5. **Screen or object demo.** Voice-over while showing something you made or use.
6. **Myth vs reality.** "People say X. In practice, Y."
7. **Day in the life, compressed.** Ten seconds of footage with a captioned point.
8. **Question I got.** Answer a real question from a friend or comment.
9. **Work in progress.** Show the thing, the hard part, the next step.
10. **Reaction with a point.** React to a common idea or short clip you have rights to show, and add your view.

You do not need all ten. Choose three that fit your personality and setup. If you hate being on screen all the time, lean on formats 5, 7 and 9. If you love talking, lean on 1, 3 and 6.

## Building your menu

Make a table with pillars as columns and your chosen formats as rows. In each cell, write one idea. With 4 pillars and 3 formats, you have 12 ideas on one page. That is almost two weeks of content.

| | Journey | Lessons | How it works | Opinions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Talking head | idea | idea | idea | idea |
| Three things | idea | idea | idea | idea |
| Work in progress | idea | idea | idea | idea |

## A weekly rhythm

Once you are publishing (week 3 onward), a simple balance is: one video per pillar per week, rotating formats. You do not have to follow it, but it prevents the feed from becoming one repeated idea.

## Example

Pillar: *Opinions.* Format: *Myth vs reality.*
Idea: "People say you should learn one programming language deeply. In practice, I learned more from reading other people's code in three languages."
Structure: hook with the myth, one concrete example, the turn ("the thing that mattered wasn't the language"), the payoff.

## Common mistakes

* **Too many pillars.** More than four dilutes the thread.
* **Format hopping.** Changing the format every video wastes the benefit of repetition.
* **Formats you dislike.** If it makes you dread filming, drop it.
* **Pillars without a viewer.** Check that each pillar answers a real question someone might have.

## Exercise

1. Write your 3-4 pillars.
2. Choose three formats.
3. Fill the table with at least 12 ideas.
4. Mark the five you are most excited to film. Move them to your idea inbox (lesson 3.1).

## Daily practice

* **Minimum (keeps the streak):** a voice note of any length.
* **Today's take (about 30 seconds):** Pitch one idea from your pillar/format table as if it were a real reel.
* Weeks 1-2 everything stays private; from week 3 follow your publishing plan.
* Don't watch the take right away (lesson 4.1). Tick the day in your tracker.

# Lesson 9.1: Monetization models

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/09-business/01-monetization-models.md)

**Time:** 40 minutes

## Goal

Understand the main ways creators earn money, which of them fit a personal-brand channel, and in what order to consider them, without rushing into them before you have an audience.

## First, the honest picture

Money from short-video content comes from audience trust and attention. Neither grows quickly. Many creators earn little or nothing from content for a long time, and many treat it as a complement to a job or business rather than a replacement. This course's approach: **build the habit and the audience first; monetize when your views are stable and you understand your viewers.** Monetizing too early can damage the trust that makes it work later.

## The main models

### 1. Brand partnerships (sponsored content)

A brand pays you, or gives you products or services, to make content about them. Rates vary widely and depend on your reach, your engagement, your audience and your niche. Deals can be one-off or long-term. Requirements:

* Disclosure of the commercial relationship (lesson 9.2).
* A contract or written agreement: scope, deliverables, deadlines, usage rights, payment terms (lesson 9.3).

Works best when the brand fits your thread and you would use the product anyway.

### 2. Platform programs

Platforms offer bonuses, subscriptions, gifts or revenue sharing under certain conditions (eligibility, region, follower or view thresholds). For Instagram, [Buffer's overview](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-account-type/) lists monetization (bonuses and subscriptions) among Creator-account features. Programs, names and eligibility change often and vary by country, so check your app's current settings. Don't build a plan that depends on one program existing.

### 3. Your own services

If your content shows what you know, people may hire you: consulting, freelance work, coaching, speaking, workshops. For many personal-brand creators this is the most reliable route because it uses a skill you already have, and a reel works as a trust-building sample.

### 4. Your own products

Digital products (templates, guides, courses, tools) or physical products. Requires an audience that wants the specific thing. Test demand before you build: ask in comments, run a waitlist, offer a small version first.

### 5. Affiliate links

You earn a commission when people buy through your link. It must be disclosed. Only recommend things you have used and believe in.

### 6. Community and memberships

A paid group, newsletter or membership for people who want more. Needs consistent delivery and sufficient demand. It fits the idea of a small, devoted audience in Kevin Kelly's [1000 True Fans](https://kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans/): a modest number of people who will pay directly for what you make.

### 7. Indirect benefits

Content can bring job offers, speaking invitations, collaborations, press, and a network. For many people these are worth more than direct revenue.

## A sensible order

1. **Weeks 1-4 (this course):** habit and skill.
2. **Months 2-3:** audience understanding. Which topics lead to follows and shares?
3. **When views are steady:** consider services or a small product that fits your thread.
4. **Then:** brand partnerships, if they match.
5. **Throughout:** keep your day job or main income until the content income is stable and larger than your needs.

## Matching models to your thread

Ask for each model:

* Does it fit my thread and my audience?
* Do I have the time to deliver it?
* Would I be comfortable if my audience saw exactly how I earn from them?
* Does it need legal or tax setup?

Pick one model to explore, not five.

## Warning signs

* Promises of quick riches from content.
* Anyone asking you to pay a fee to receive a "brand deal".
* Pressure to buy a program that promises a viral guarantee.
* Brands that want you to hide that it is an ad.
* Contracts that take your content rights forever for little payment.

## Common mistakes

* **Monetizing before you know your audience.**
* **Taking any deal for money.** Misfit deals cost trust.
* **Underpricing** because you're afraid of losing the deal (see lesson 9.3).
* **Ignoring paperwork.**
* **Forgetting that your time is a cost.**

## Exercise

1. Rate each model on a scale of 1-5 for fit, effort and appeal.
2. Choose one to explore, and write the smallest experiment: for example, offering a 30-minute paid consultation to three people, or testing a simple template.
3. Write your red lines: kinds of brands or products you won't promote.

## Daily practice

* **Minimum (keeps the streak):** a voice note of any length.
* **Today's take (about 30 seconds):** Explain one monetization model in plain words, as to a ten-year-old.
* Weeks 1-2 everything stays private; from week 3 follow your publishing plan.
* Don't watch the take right away (lesson 4.1). Tick the day in your tracker.

## Checklist

* [ ] I picked one model to explore.
* [ ] I wrote my red lines.

# Lesson 5.5: B-roll, shot lists and batching

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/05-filming/05-broll-and-batching.md)

**Time:** 45 minutes

## Goal

Add visual variety with b-roll, plan shots with a simple shot list, and film several reels in one session, so that consistency costs less time.

## What is b-roll?

B-roll is any footage that is not the main talking shot: your hands working, the street, your screen, objects, a close-up of a detail. It is used to illustrate what you say, to hide cuts, and to keep the eye busy. A talking video cut with b-roll feels more dynamic than the same video with only your face.

Rule: **b-roll should support the words.** If you say "I opened the laptop at midnight", show the laptop, not a random sunset.

## Types of b-roll

* **Action:** hands typing, pouring coffee, walking, writing.
* **Detail:** close-ups of objects: a notebook, a key, a screen.
* **Place:** a short wide of the location.
* **Screen recordings:** if you talk about an app or a document (make sure there is nothing private on screen).
* **Text and graphics:** simple captions or number cards.

Shoot b-roll in short clips of 3-5 seconds, at least a second longer than you need, so you can trim.

## The shot list

A shot list is a short checklist of every clip you need for a reel. It saves you from realizing in the edit that you missed something.

Example for a 25-second reel about checking email twice a day:

| # | Shot | Type | Length | Done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hook, to camera | main | 4 s | [ ] |
| 2 | Hands closing laptop | b-roll | 3 s | [ ] |
| 3 | Beat 1, to camera | main | 6 s | [ ] |
| 4 | Phone with notification badge | b-roll | 3 s | [ ] |
| 5 | Beat 2, to camera | main | 6 s | [ ] |
| 6 | Payoff, to camera | main | 4 s | [ ] |

Before filming, list the shots for each reel. While filming, tick them. This takes five minutes and prevents re-shoots.

## Batching

Batching means doing the same kind of task for several reels at once. It is the main trick for consistency.

### Batch ideas and scripts

Once a week, choose 3-5 concepts and write their beat sheets (lessons 3.3 and 3.5).

### Batch filming

Pick a session of 60-90 minutes. Steps:

1. Prepare: clean lens, lock exposure, charge, check sound.
2. Film all the **main takes** first, one reel after another. Change shirt or location only if you want to vary the look between reels.
3. Film all the **b-roll** afterward, in one sweep, to save time.
4. Record a **room tone** clip.
5. Back up the footage to your computer or cloud.

Batching works because setup is the expensive part. Once the light, mic and mood are right, each extra reel costs little.

### Batch editing and posting

Edit all reels in a block, then schedule or post across days. This is covered in lessons 6.1 and 7.1.

## Naming and organizing

Messy files cost time. Use a simple scheme:

* Folder per reel: `reel-03_email-twice-a-day`
* Inside: `raw/`, `broll/`, `audio/`, `export/`
* File names: `A_hook_01.mov`, `B_beat1_02.mov` where letters are order and numbers are takes.

Or simply note the best take number in your shot list so you can find it quickly.

## Keep it simple at first

You don't need b-roll for every reel. A clean talking head with good captions is a valid format. Add b-roll when it helps the content, not because it is expected.

## Common mistakes

* **Filming b-roll without a purpose.**
* **Too short b-roll clips** that you can't trim.
* **Shooting everything on the same day** without a plan, producing lots of footage you never use.
* **Forgetting to back up.**
* **Filming under inconsistent light between batches.**

## Exercise

1. Choose two concepts from lesson 3.3.
2. Write a shot list for each.
3. Do a 60-minute batch: film mains for both, then b-roll for both, then room tone.
4. Name and file the footage using your scheme.

## Daily practice

* **Minimum (keeps the streak):** a voice note of any length.
* **Today's take (about 30 seconds):** Film one main take plus two 3-second b-roll clips for it.
* Weeks 1-2 everything stays private; from week 3 follow your publishing plan.
* Don't watch the take right away (lesson 4.1). Tick the day in your tracker.

## Checklist

* [ ] I know what b-roll is and when to use it.
* [ ] I wrote a shot list before filming.
* [ ] I filmed at least two reels in one session.
* [ ] My footage is named and backed up.
* [ ] I recorded today's voice note and take.

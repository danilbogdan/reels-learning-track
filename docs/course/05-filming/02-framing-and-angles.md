# Lesson 5.2: Framing and angles

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/05-filming/02-framing-and-angles.md)

**Time:** 45 minutes

## Goal

Learn to frame a vertical shot, choose angles on purpose, and film the same idea from several angles so your reels look varied without needing more equipment.

## The vertical frame

Reels and TikToks are vertical, usually 9:16 (for example 1080 by 1920 pixels). Film in vertical from the start. Cropping a landscape video to vertical wastes resolution and often cuts important things.

### Safe zones

Platform interfaces overlay text on parts of your video: the username and caption at the bottom, buttons on the right edge, a top bar. Specific measurements change with app updates, so test with your own account: upload a test video privately (if the platform allows a private or draft state) and see what is covered. As a general rule:

* Keep faces and key text **away from the bottom 20 percent** and **the right edge**.
* Keep the top 10 percent mostly empty.
* Put captions in the middle-lower area, not the very bottom.

### Headroom and eye line

* Leave a small gap above your head, roughly the space of a hand's width.
* Put your eyes about one-third from the top of the frame. This follows the rule of thirds and is a natural, pleasant position.
* Center yourself horizontally for a direct feel, or offset slightly to leave room for on-screen text.

### Distance

* **Close-up** (shoulders and head): intimate, good for opinions and stories.
* **Medium** (waist up): shows gestures, good for tips.
* **Wide** (full body or scene): sets context, good for b-roll and walking shots.

Closer is usually better for talking head; it fills the small screen and makes your expression visible.

## Camera height and angle

Angle changes how the viewer feels.

* **Eye level:** neutral, honest. The default.
* **Slightly above eye level:** flattering, common for selfies. Avoid too high: it makes you look small.
* **Below eye level looking up:** powerful or dramatic; can distort the face if close.
* **Over the shoulder:** shows what you see; good for demos.
* **Top-down:** shows hands and objects; good for desk, cooking, writing.

Rule: pick the angle for the feeling you want, and keep it for the duration of a beat.

## Five angles, one idea

Film a single 20-second idea five ways, in one 30-minute session:

1. **Front, eye level:** classic talking head.
2. **Low:** phone on a table angled up.
3. **Side or three-quarter:** you at an angle to the lens, looking at it.
4. **Over the shoulder / screen:** the camera sees your work.
5. **Walking:** the phone held at chest height, steady.

Review all five. Note which feel strongest for which type of idea. Over time you will have a small repertoire of angles that fit your content.

## Changing framing inside one reel

A cut between framings keeps a talking video lively. Two simple approaches:

* **Punch-in:** film in 4K or crop in editing by 10-20 percent at selected moments (for emphasis). It looks like a second camera.
* **Two-take method:** film the same lines twice, once wider, once closer, and alternate between them in the edit.

Don't change the framing in the middle of a sentence unless the cut is planned.

## Backgrounds

* **Declutter.** The viewer should look at you, not at the shelf.
* **Add depth.** A background a meter or two behind you looks better than a wall behind your shoulders.
* **Check what's visible.** Documents, screens, names, windows and addresses (see lesson 2.3).
* **Keep it consistent,** if you want a recognisable look, or vary on purpose.

## Stability

Shaky footage reads as amateur, and it can make viewers uncomfortable.

* Use a tripod or lean the phone on something solid.
* When handheld, keep your elbows close to the body and move smoothly.
* Use stabilization modes only if you need them.

## Common mistakes

* **Cut-off foreheads.** Check the top edge.
* **Too far away.** Your face becomes small.
* **Subjects too low,** with empty ceiling above.
* **Text in the bottom bar.**
* **Moving the camera while talking** without need.

## Exercise

1. Film one 20-second idea in the five angles.
2. Compare them in a batch review and write which angle fits which idea.
3. Check the safe zones by uploading a private test video or by overlaying a rough mask on a screenshot.
4. Re-film one take with a better background.

## Daily practice

* **Minimum (keeps the streak):** a voice note of any length.
* **Today's take (about 30 seconds):** Film from a different angle than yesterday.
* Weeks 1-2 everything stays private; from week 3 follow your publishing plan.
* Don't watch the take right away (lesson 4.1). Tick the day in your tracker.

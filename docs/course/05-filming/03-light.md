# Lesson 5.3: Light

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/05-filming/03-light.md)

**Time:** 40 minutes

## Goal

Get clean, flattering light without special equipment, and know what to buy first if you ever want to improve it.

## Why light matters more than the camera

A phone with good light beats a better camera with bad light. Light controls how sharp, clean and professional the image looks. Poor light causes grain, color shifts and a flat or harsh appearance. Fortunately, good light is mostly about where you stand.

## The basics

### Direction

* **Face the light source.** Your face should be lit from the front or at about 45 degrees to the side. Backlit (light behind you) leaves your face in shadow.
* **Window light is the best free light.** Position yourself facing a window, or turned slightly so the window is at a 45-degree angle. Place the phone so it is between you and the window or beside the window.
* **Avoid light from directly above.** Overhead lights create shadows in the eye sockets.
* **Avoid light from below.** It looks unnatural.

### Soft versus hard

* **Soft light** (overcast sky, a window with a sheer curtain) makes gentle shadows and looks flattering.
* **Hard light** (direct sun, bare bulb) creates sharp shadows and highlights every blemish.

If direct sun hits your face, move back or hang a thin white fabric over the window.

### Color temperature

Different lights have different color: daylight is bluish-white, warm bulbs are orange. Mixed lights (a window plus a warm lamp) produce uneven color on your skin and wall.

* Use **one type of light** on your face. Turn off room lamps that conflict with daylight.
* If you must mix, keep the main light on your face and let the others be background.
* Lock **white balance** if your camera app allows, to avoid color shift during the take.

### Brightness consistency

Lighting that changes during a take (passing clouds, shifting sun) makes editing difficult. Film in a stable window time, and batch your shoots when light is best, often mid-morning or late afternoon.

## Free tools you already own

* **A window.** Primary light source.
* **A white sheet or paper.** Hold it opposite the window to bounce light into the shadow side.
* **A lamp with a white shade.** For evenings. Place it at 45 degrees in front.
* **Curtains.** To diffuse or block sun.

## Evening and indoor

If you film after dark, a single soft lamp in front of you at face height is far better than the ceiling light. Use a white bulb (neutral, around 4000-5000 K if you can choose) to avoid yellow skin. Turn off conflicting lights.

## If you want to buy something

In this order:

1. A **small LED panel or ring light** with adjustable brightness and color temperature. Soft, even, easy.
2. A **diffuser** or softbox for the panel.
3. A **second light** or a reflector for shadow fill.
4. **Practical lights** (lamps or LED strips) behind you for background depth.

Don't buy anything until you've tried a window for a week. You'll know what you actually miss.

## A quick light test

Before you film, take a 5-second test and look:

* Is your face brighter than the background?
* Are there dark shadows under your eyes or nose?
* Are your skin tones natural, not orange or blue?
* Does the light stay stable through the take?

If not, move a half-meter and test again. Small moves make large differences.

## Examples

* **Good:** you, seated facing a large window, phone at eye level, soft overcast light.
* **Bad:** you with your back to a bright window; the camera exposes for the window and you become a silhouette.
* **Fixable:** a window to the side with a sheet of white paper on the opposite side to reflect light into the shadow.

## Common mistakes

* **Backlit by a window.**
* **Mixed color lights.**
* **Overhead-only light.**
* **Changing light mid-session** and then trying to match takes.
* **Buying gear before learning placement.**

## Exercise

1. Film the same 10-second line in five positions: facing the window, side to the window, back to the window, under a ceiling light, with a lamp in front.
2. Compare in a batch. Choose your default position.
3. Mark the spot on the floor with tape and note the best time of day.
4. Try the sheet-of-paper reflector and see the effect on shadows.

## Checklist

* [ ] I know my best light spot at home.
* [ ] My face is lit from the front or 45 degrees.
* [ ] I avoid mixed color lights.
* [ ] I know when the light is best during the day.

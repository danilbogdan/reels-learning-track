# Lesson 5.1: Phone setup (iPhone as the example)

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/05-filming/01-iphone-setup.md)

**Time:** 40 minutes

## Goal

Configure your phone once, so every video you film is clean, compatible with your editing tools and consistent. This lesson uses an iPhone as the example; the principles apply to any phone, and menu names vary by model and operating system version, so check your own device.

## The setup decisions

### 1. Recording format

On iPhone: **Settings > Camera > Formats**. Apple describes two options ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT207022)):

* **High Efficiency:** uses HEVC (H.265) for video and HEIF for photos. Files are smaller at similar quality.
* **Most Compatible:** uses H.264 for video and JPEG for photos. Better compatibility with older software and devices.

Which to choose? If you edit with phone apps, High Efficiency is usually fine. If you edit with free desktop tools or command-line tools like ffmpeg and have run into decoding problems, start with **Most Compatible**. It removes one common source of trouble. You trade file size for fewer surprises.

Apple also notes that when you import to a computer, files may be converted to compatible formats. To keep the original files, set the Photos transfer option to keep originals (in Photos settings, the option is called "Keep Originals" in the transfer section).

### 2. Resolution and frame rate

On iPhone: **Settings > Camera > Record Video**. Apple's default is 30 frames per second, and depending on the model other choices are available ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/change-video-recording-settings-iphc1827d32f/15.0/ios)).

Practical choice for reels:

* **Resolution:** 1080p or 4K. 4K gives you room to crop and punch in during editing but produces larger files. Instagram and TikTok export at around 1080x1920, so 4K is a luxury, not a necessity. Start with 1080p if storage or speed is a problem.
* **Frame rate:** 30 fps for a natural, standard look. 60 fps for smoother motion and slow motion; it doubles the data. Avoid mixing frame rates in a single reel, because it complicates editing.

### 3. Variable frame rate warning

Phone video is often recorded with a **variable frame rate (VFR)**: the phone varies the number of frames per second depending on scene and conditions. Many editing tools handle this poorly, which causes audio and video to drift apart or stutter. This is general practice knowledge, not an official Apple statement. The fix is to convert footage to a constant frame rate before editing (lesson 6.1).

### 4. Lock exposure and focus

Auto exposure and focus can shift mid-take as you move your hands, causing brightness to pulse. On iPhone, tap and hold on your face (or the spot where it will be) until **AE/AF Lock** appears. This keeps focus and exposure fixed.

### 5. Lens choice

* **Main (1x) lens** is the best all-round choice for talking head. It usually has the best sensor.
* **Ultra-wide** exaggerates distance and distorts faces near the edge. Use it for scenes, not close-ups.
* **Telephoto** (if available) flattens and makes a face look better at a distance.
* **Selfie camera** is more convenient but typically lower quality; use the rear camera when you can, with a way to check framing.

### 6. Features to turn on or off

* **Grid:** turn it on (Settings > Camera > Grid). It helps with framing.
* **Audio:** make sure the microphone is not blocked by a case or finger.
* **Stabilization / action mode:** useful when walking; can crop the image.
* **Live effects and beauty filters:** avoid them at first; they can add artifacts.
* **HDR video:** it can look fine on your phone but harsh when converted. If you see weird colors after export, turn it off.
* **Do Not Disturb:** enable it so calls and notifications do not interrupt.

### 7. Storage and battery

* Keep at least 10 GB free before a filming session.
* Charge to above 50 percent. Phones heat up when filming and may reduce performance.
* If the phone overheats, rest it in shade.

## Clean the lens

The single easiest quality upgrade: wipe the lens with a soft cloth before you film. Fingerprints create haze and flares.

## Mounting

* A small tripod or phone grip stabilizes the shot. A pile of books works too.
* Place the phone at eye level or slightly above.
* Use a timer or a Bluetooth remote, or a voice command, to start without touching the phone.

## Your setup note

Write a short "camera recipe" you can reuse. Example:

* Rear main lens, vertical, 1080p, 30 fps
* Format: Most Compatible
* AE/AF Lock on face
* Phone at eye level, 1.2 m away
* Do Not Disturb on
* 10 GB free

Keep the recipe in your notes and check it before each session.

## Common mistakes

* **Changing settings every day.** Consistency matters more than the "best" setting.
* **Filming in landscape** and cropping later.
* **Forgetting to lock exposure.**
* **Leaving a case over the microphone.**

## Exercise

1. Open your camera settings and write down your current format, resolution and frame rate.
2. Choose your recipe and write it down.
3. Film a 15-second test take with the recipe. Check for brightness pulses, focus changes and clear audio.
4. Transfer the file to your editing device and check it plays correctly.

## Daily practice

* **Minimum (keeps the streak):** a voice note of any length.
* **Today's take (about 30 seconds):** Film using your camera recipe. Check that exposure and focus stay locked.
* Weeks 1-2 everything stays private; from week 3 follow your publishing plan.
* Don't watch the take right away (lesson 4.1). Tick the day in your tracker.

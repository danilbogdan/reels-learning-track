# Lesson 3.1: Capture: the idea inbox and voice notes

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/03-ideas/01-capture-voice-notes.md)

**Time:** 30 minutes setup, then 5 minutes daily

## Goal

Build a frictionless way to capture ideas, thoughts and stories as they appear, so you never need to "think of something" when you sit down to film.

## The idea

Good short videos are made from specific moments: something you noticed, a thought you had on a walk, a small realization. These moments are fleeting. If you rely on memory, you will have the thought in the shower and a blank mind at your desk. The fix is a **capture habit**: a single place where raw material lands, with almost no effort.

Voice notes are the best capture tool for most people because they are fast, they keep your natural speech, and they train the exact skill you need: saying a thought out loud.

## Build your system in three parts

### 1. One inbox

Choose one place for all ideas. It can be a notes app, a voice-memo folder, a markdown file or a paper notebook. The rule: **if you have more than one inbox, you have none.** Put a shortcut on your phone's home screen so it takes one tap.

### 2. Daily voice note

Record one voice note a day. Length: 30 seconds to two minutes. Content: anything from your day. Prompts if you are stuck:

* What surprised me today?
* What did I get wrong, and what did it teach me?
* What did I explain to someone, and how?
* What am I avoiding, and why?
* What did I see or read that I disagree with?

Do not edit. Do not judge. Talk as you would to a friend. This note is your **minimum day**: if you do nothing else, you keep your streak.

### 3. Weekly harvest

Once a week (a 20-minute session), go through the notes and pull out candidates. For each promising note, write one line in your idea list: *the claim or story in a sentence*. Mark the best three. These become your next week's reels.

A simple idea-card template:

* **Line:** the idea in one sentence.
* **Pillar:** which pillar it belongs to.
* **Format:** which format fits.
* **Why would someone care?** One line.
* **Status:** raw / ready to script / filmed.

## Transcribing

If your phone or computer can transcribe voice notes, use it. A transcript makes it easy to find the sentence that could be a hook. Many people find that the best hook is sitting in the third sentence of their rambling note. If you use a transcription tool, check what it does with your audio and where the text is stored, so that private material stays private.

## Example

Voice note, Tuesday evening: "I almost didn't open the laptop today. I've been putting off a task for two weeks, and when I finally started it took twenty minutes. I keep thinking the avoiding is the real work..."

Harvest line: "Avoiding a task costs more than doing it: two weeks of dread for twenty minutes of work." Pillar: lessons. Format: story in the middle. Hook: "I avoided a twenty-minute task for two weeks."

## Rules that keep it alive

* **Capture first, judge later.** No filter in the inbox.
* **Never delete ideas in the moment.** Weak ideas often combine into strong ones.
* **Keep notes short.** Detail comes when you script.
* **Record in the moment where possible.** Walks and commutes are good for this.
* **Do not turn it into a project.** A fancy system you maintain is a distraction.

## Common mistakes

* **Setting a high bar for notes.** The inbox is not for finished ideas.
* **Capturing but never harvesting.** An unread inbox is a graveyard.
* **Writing instead of talking.** Writing tempts you to polish.
* **Overbuilding the tool.** A phone voice recorder plus one note file is enough.

## Exercise

1. Create your inbox and put a shortcut on your phone.
2. Record today's voice note, 60 seconds.
3. List 10 ideas already in your head and put them in the inbox.
4. Schedule the weekly harvest in your calendar.

## Checklist

* [ ] I have one inbox and a one-tap shortcut.
* [ ] I recorded a voice note today.
* [ ] I have at least 10 raw ideas in the inbox.
* [ ] My weekly harvest is scheduled.

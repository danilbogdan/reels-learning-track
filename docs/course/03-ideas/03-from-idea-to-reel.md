# Lesson 3.3: From idea to reel

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/03-ideas/03-from-idea-to-reel.md)

**Time:** 45 minutes

## Goal

Take a raw idea from your inbox and turn it into a clear, filmable concept: one sentence, one viewer, one promise, one ending.

## Ideas are not concepts

"Productivity" is a topic. "Why I stopped using to-do lists" is an idea. "I replaced my to-do list with a single question every morning, and here's what happened in a month" is a concept. A concept contains a viewer, a promise, a shape and an ending. Most abandoned videos were never concepts; they were ideas that stayed vague, and vague ideas are hard to film.

## The concept test: five questions

Answer these in writing for any idea you want to film.

1. **Who is this for?** One specific kind of person. "Anyone" is no one.
2. **What do they want or fear?** The tension that makes them stay.
3. **What is my one claim or story?** A single sentence. If it has "and" in it, you may have two videos.
4. **Why am I the person to say this?** Your experience, evidence or perspective. This is your credibility.
5. **What do they do or feel at the end?** Learn something, laugh, feel seen, try something.

If you cannot answer all five in under five minutes, the idea is not ready. Park it and pick another.

## Find the angle

An angle is a specific way into a topic. The same topic can have many angles:

Topic: *Waking up early.*

* Personal story: "I tried 5 a.m. for 30 days. I quit on day 9."
* Contrarian: "Waking up early made me worse at my job."
* Practical: "The one change that made mornings easier."
* Myth: "You don't need a morning routine. You need a stopping time."

Generate at least three angles for every idea and choose the one that is (a) true for you, (b) specific, and (c) a bit surprising.

## Prove it with a detail

Abstract statements slide past people. A specific detail stays. Add one or two concrete details to every concept: a number, a name of a tool, a quote, a place, a moment ("it was 11 p.m.", "my laptop had 3 percent battery").

* Weak: "I felt overwhelmed at work."
* Strong: "I had forty browser tabs open and couldn't remember which one I was supposed to be working on."

Specifics are also what make a video feel like *you* and not like any other creator.

## Check the cost

Before you film, estimate:

* **Setup:** where and how will I shoot? (Same place as usual is best.)
* **Time:** can I film and edit this in under an hour?
* **Complexity:** how many shots, props, locations?

Early on, choose concepts that are cheap to make. Complexity is a common reason people stop.

## Example

Raw idea in the inbox: "I check email too much."

* Who: people who feel busy but unproductive.
* Want/fear: they want focus, fear falling behind.
* Claim: "Checking email less often made me faster at replying."
* Why me: I tried a one-month experiment and kept notes.
* End: they try a two-check rule for a week.
* Angle (chosen): contrarian + personal.
* Hook: "I check my email twice a day and I reply faster than ever."
* Promise: "Here's why."
* Beats: batching reduces switching; one-hour windows; the setup.
* Turn: "The surprise: people noticed less delay, not more."
* Payoff: "Try two checks a day for a week."
* Cost: talking head, 25 seconds, ten minutes to film.

## Common mistakes

* **Starting to film without a payoff.** You will ramble.
* **Covering a topic instead of making a point.**
* **Choosing a high-cost concept as your first.**
* **Ignoring the viewer.** Make one video for one person.

## Exercise

Take five ideas from your inbox. For each, write the five questions and three angles. Choose the best angle for each. Select the **two cheapest** concepts, write their five parts, and mark them ready for scripting (lesson 3.5).

## Daily practice

* **Minimum (keeps the streak):** a voice note of any length.
* **Today's take (about 30 seconds):** Deliver one concept from the five-question test, from hook to payoff.
* Weeks 1-2 everything stays private; from week 3 follow your publishing plan.
* Don't watch the take right away (lesson 4.1). Tick the day in your tracker.

# Lesson 3.5: Scripting without sounding scripted

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/03-ideas/05-scripting-without-a-script.md)

**Time:** 45 minutes

## Goal

Learn a way to plan what you will say that gives you structure and word economy, but still sounds like a person talking.

## The tension

A full script keeps you on track but tends to sound stiff, especially if you read it or memorize it. No plan at all leads to rambling. The solution is a middle path: **write talking points, rehearse them out loud, and let the exact words change.**

## Beat sheet

A beat sheet is a list of beats, each a few words long. It is your map.

Format:

1. **Hook:** exact words (you want these fixed).
2. **Promise:** a few words.
3. **Beat 1:** idea + detail to mention.
4. **Beat 2:** idea + detail.
5. **Beat 3:** idea + detail.
6. **Turn:** the one surprising line.
7. **Payoff:** exact words (fixed).

Fix the first and last lines. Keep the middle flexible. The ending is where many videos fade, so write it out.

## Word budget

Normal speech runs at about 130-160 words per minute, which is roughly 65-80 words in 30 seconds. This is a rule of thumb; your pace may differ. To find yours: set a timer, read a paragraph aloud naturally, count the words.

Plan to be a little under budget; you will add words when you speak. A 30-second reel with 60 planned words usually fits. If your plan is 150 words, either split it into two reels or cut ruthlessly.

## Writing for the ear

Spoken language is not written language.

* Use short sentences. One idea each.
* Use contractions ("don't", "it's").
* Prefer simple words. "Use" over "utilize".
* Repeat on purpose. Repetition is natural in speech.
* Avoid lists of more than three items.
* Avoid long subordinate clauses; the listener cannot re-read.
* Use "you" more than "one".

Test: read your lines aloud. Wherever you stumble or run out of breath, rewrite.

## The three-pass method

1. **Brain dump (2 min).** Say the idea out loud, as a voice note, with no plan. Capture the raw version.
2. **Highlight (5 min).** Listen back or read the transcript. Mark the best sentences. The strongest ones are often already there.
3. **Rebuild (10 min).** Arrange the best sentences into the five-part structure and fill the gaps. Trim.

This approach starts from your natural speech and keeps it.

## Rehearsal

* Say it aloud three times, without reading.
* Change words freely; keep the order of beats.
* On the third pass, say it while standing, looking at a point, as you will in front of the camera.
* Then record.

Do not memorize the exact sentences, except the hook and the last line.

## Teleprompter or notes?

* **Notes in your head** (beat sheet) work best for most people.
* **Notes just out of frame** can help for lists or numbers, but eye movement may show.
* **Teleprompter apps** give a polished look but require practice to avoid a reading voice. If you try one, keep sentences short and speed slow.

Start without, and add only if you need it.

## Handling mistakes

* If you stumble, **pause and restart the sentence**. A clean cut later will remove it ([lesson 6.2](../06-editing/02-cutting-for-pace.md)).
* Do not stop the whole take for one error.
* Leave a beat of silence before the retake so the edit is easy.

## Example: beat sheet

Concept: *Checking email twice a day.*

1. Hook (fixed): "I check email twice a day and reply faster than ever."
2. Promise: "Here's the setup."
3. Beat 1: batching stops switching; one big window.
4. Beat 2: auto-reply that sets expectations.
5. Beat 3: the first week was awkward, then calm.
6. Turn: nobody complained, one person thanked me.
7. Payoff (fixed): "Try two checks for a week. If it fails, you lose nothing."

Spoken, this is about 65 words and 28 seconds.

## Common mistakes

* **Writing an essay.** Essays are for reading.
* **Over-memorizing.** It makes your eyes go blank.
* **Under-preparing the ending.**
* **Never timing a read-through.**

## Exercise

1. Take one concept from lesson 3.3.
2. Do the three-pass method: dump, highlight, rebuild.
3. Write the beat sheet with fixed hook and payoff.
4. Count the words and time yourself.
5. Rehearse three times and record one take. Do not watch it yet.

## Daily practice

* **Minimum (keeps the streak):** a voice note of any length.
* **Today's take (about 30 seconds):** Deliver your beat sheet without notes, with the fixed hook and payoff.
* Weeks 1-2 everything stays private; from week 3 follow your publishing plan.
* Don't watch the take right away (lesson 4.1). Tick the day in your tracker.

# Lesson 1.2: Anatomy of a reel

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/01-how-it-works/02-anatomy-of-a-reel.md)

**Time:** 35 minutes

## Goal

Learn a simple structure that fits almost any short video, so that you never face a blank page: **hook, promise, body, turn, payoff.**

## Why structure helps

A short video has seconds to earn attention and seconds to keep it. If you improvise the shape, you will ramble, bury the point or lose the viewer in the middle. A reusable skeleton frees your attention for what you actually want to say. Structure is not a cage; it is a lane.

## The five parts

### 1. Hook (0-3 seconds)

The first line and first frame. Its job is to make the right person think "this is for me" and "I want to know the end." It is not an introduction. "Hi, I'm Alex and today I want to talk about..." is not a hook.

A hook usually does one of these: states a surprising claim, names a problem the viewer has, opens a question, shows an intriguing result, or starts a story in the middle.

### 2. Promise (the next 2-3 seconds)

Tell the viewer what they will get and, implicitly, how long it will take. "Here are the three habits that changed how I work." A promise lets the viewer commit. It also gives you a rule for editing: anything that does not serve the promise goes.

For very short reels the hook and promise are one sentence.

### 3. Body (the middle)

The content that fulfils the promise. Keep it in small, clear beats. Beats are units of about 3-8 seconds, each with one idea. A new beat is a natural place to change the framing, add a cut, or show a caption. Do not stack three ideas in one beat.

### 4. Turn (the unexpected bit)

A turn is the moment the video changes direction: a twist, a contradiction, a "but here is the thing". It keeps the middle from feeling like a list. Not every reel needs one, but even tutorials benefit from a small surprise, like "the mistake that actually mattered was the last one".

### 5. Payoff (the end)

The payoff delivers on the promise. It can be the answer, the punchline, the lesson, or the emotional point. Then stop. Do not append a long outro, a recap or "thanks for watching". Strong endings often **loop** back to the opening line, which makes the video easy to replay.

A call to action, if you use one, should be short and specific, and should come after the payoff, not before.

## Four common shapes

All of these use the five parts. Pick the shape before you start.

1. **Story:** a moment, a problem, what happened, what it taught you. (Hook is the moment in the middle of the action.)
2. **Opinion:** a claim, why you believe it, the strongest counterargument, your conclusion.
3. **How-to:** the result, the steps, the common mistake, the final tip.
4. **Behind the scenes:** a thing in progress, the hard part, how you handled it, where it stands.

## Length

There is no official perfect length. Practice suggests that short is easier to finish and that every second must earn its place. A useful starting range is **15-40 seconds**. Your own retention numbers (lesson 7.2) will tell you better.

A rule of thumb for scripting: normal speech runs at roughly 130-160 words per minute, which is about 65-80 words in 30 seconds. If your script is much longer, cut it.

## Worked example

Idea: *I used to think good engineers never ask for help.*

* **Hook:** "For ten years I thought asking for help meant I was bad at my job."
* **Promise:** "Here's what changed my mind."
* **Body:** Beat 1: the day a stuck problem cost the team a week. Beat 2: the colleague who asked a "dumb" question and unblocked everyone.
* **Turn:** "The smartest person in that room asked the simplest question."
* **Payoff:** "Asking early is not weakness. It is the fastest way to be right."

Total about 70 words, around 30 seconds.

## Common mistakes

* **A slow warm-up.** Cut every sentence before the real start.
* **Several promises.** One reel, one promise.
* **A flat middle.** Without beats and a turn, the middle is where people leave.
* **A weak ending.** If you cannot say what the payoff is, you do not have a video yet.

## Exercise

Take three videos you finished watching in lesson 1.1. For each, mark: the hook (what was the first line?), the promise, the beats, the turn and the payoff. Write them in a small table. Then pick one of your own ideas and write the five parts as five short lines. Do not film it yet.

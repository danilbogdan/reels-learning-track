# Lesson 4.3: Eyes, face, hands and posture

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/04-on-camera/03-eyes-face-hands.md)

**Time:** 35 minutes

## Goal

Control the visual side of your delivery: where you look, how your face moves, what your hands do and how you sit or stand, so the picture supports the words.

## Eye line

Where you look decides whether a viewer feels addressed.

* **Look at the lens, not at yourself on the screen.** This is the most common mistake. The preview on the screen is a trap. When you look at your image, your eyes land slightly below the lens and you appear to look away.
* **Tape a small marker next to the lens** (a sticky dot or a tiny photo) as a target.
* **Use the rear camera when possible.** It has a better sensor, and without a screen to look at you naturally look at the lens. Use a small mirror or a monitor placed beside the phone if you need to check framing.
* **Look away on purpose, sometimes.** A glance up or sideways to "think" is natural between beats. Return to the lens for key lines, especially the hook and payoff.
* **Don't read from off-screen constantly.** It breaks contact.

## Your face

* **Relax your forehead and jaw.** Tension reads as stiffness.
* **Smile when it fits.** A real smile shows in the eyes; do not hold a fixed one.
* **Let your face react to your own words.** If the line is surprising, let your eyebrows rise a bit. Slight over-expression reads as natural on a small screen.
* **Avoid "frozen face" between sentences.** Keep a light, interested look in the pauses.
* **Wear what you are comfortable in.** Plain, solid colors tend to look cleaner than busy patterns, which can shimmer on video.

## Hands

Hands are either an asset or a distraction.

* **Use them for emphasis, at chest height,** within the frame.
* **Keep gestures purposeful:** counting points on fingers for a list, showing size, pointing at on-screen text.
* **Don't flap.** Large, fast movements blur and distract.
* **If you are not sure, keep them still** (resting on a desk, holding an object) rather than fidgeting.
* **Holding a prop** (a mug, a notebook) gives hands a job.

## Posture and framing of the body

* **Sit or stand tall,** shoulders relaxed. Slouching shrinks you and your voice.
* **Lean slightly toward the camera** when making an important point; this reads as engagement.
* **Standing gives more energy,** sitting more intimacy. Choose by mood of the reel.
* **Avoid swivel chairs** that make you drift.
* **Keep your head level,** not tilted down at the phone.

## Movement

Movement adds interest, but too much hides your face.

* **Walking while talking** works for casual or story reels. Keep the phone steady (arm held close to the body or a small grip).
* **Static framing** works for focused tips.
* **Mix:** start static for the hook, move for the story, return static for the payoff.

## Common reel "tells" to avoid

* Looking at the preview on the screen.
* Reading a script with moving eyes.
* Rocking or swaying.
* Breathing out at the end of each sentence into the microphone.
* Hair or jewelry hitting the lavalier.
* Touching your face, then touching your face again.

You will notice these in review. Fix one per week; trying to fix all at once is paralyzing.

## Review with a checklist

When you watch a take (in a batch, not right away), tick:

| Question | Yes/No |
|---|---|
| Did I look at the lens on the hook? | |
| Was my energy up? | |
| Were my hands calm or purposeful? | |
| Was my posture upright? | |
| Did my face react to my words? | |

Pick **one** item to improve in tomorrow's take.

## Common mistakes

* **Watching yourself while you talk.**
* **Over-gesturing.**
* **Hiding behind a stiff posture.**
* **Ignoring eye line on the hook.**

## Exercise

1. Place a marker by the lens. Film the same 20-second line three times: looking at the screen, looking at the lens, looking at the lens with a hand gesture. Compare.
2. Record a walking take of 15 seconds with the phone held steady.
3. Use the review checklist on your last three takes and pick one item to improve.

## Daily practice

* **Minimum (keeps the streak):** a voice note of any length.
* **Today's take (about 30 seconds):** Film with the eye-line marker and your hands at chest height.
* Weeks 1-2 everything stays private; from week 3 follow your publishing plan.
* Don't watch the take right away (lesson 4.1). Tick the day in your tracker.

## Checklist

* [ ] I have an eye-line marker by the lens.
* [ ] I know where my hands go.
* [ ] I stand or sit tall and relaxed.
* [ ] I picked one thing to improve tomorrow.
* [ ] I recorded today's voice note and take.

# Lesson 4.1: Why it feels awful, and what to do about it

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/04-on-camera/01-why-it-feels-awful.md)

**Time:** 35 minutes

## Goal

Understand why speaking to a camera feels uncomfortable, and use concrete, repeatable practices that make it easier, instead of waiting for confidence to arrive.

## What is happening

If you hate how you look and sound on camera, you are normal. Several things combine:

* **You are hearing yourself from the outside.** Your voice in a recording sounds different from what you hear in your head, because you normally hear it partly through bone conduction. Almost everyone finds the recorded version strange at first.
* **You see a mirror you are not used to.** A phone camera shows you un-flipped, as others see you. Your face looks slightly different from the mirror version you know.
* **You are judging and performing at once.** Part of your attention is on what you say, part on how you look, part on imagined viewers. That divided attention shows as stiffness.
* **There is no feedback loop.** In conversation, a listener's nod or smile tells you how it is going. A lens gives nothing back.
* **It is a skill.** Speaking to a lens is a learned skill, like public speaking. The discomfort is mostly unfamiliarity, and it decreases with repetition.

None of this means you lack charisma. It means you have not yet accumulated reps.

## Principle: reps, not courage

You do not become comfortable by thinking about it. You become comfortable by seeing yourself on screen many times and discovering that nothing bad happens. The technical term is **exposure**: repeated, manageable contact with the thing that feels uncomfortable. Keep sessions short and frequent rather than long and rare.

## Six practices

### 1. Lower the stakes

Film privately in weeks 1-2. No audience. No pressure. The knowledge that nothing will be published frees you.

### 2. Do many takes and keep the last

Early takes are always stiff. Do three to five takes of each line and use the last. Do not judge takes while filming; judge them only in the edit.

### 3. Don't watch immediately

After a take, do not play it back. Self-criticism in the moment makes the next take worse. Review in batches, later, with a note-taking mindset ("pace too fast, good energy at second 8") instead of a judging mindset ("I look awful").

### 4. Talk to one person

Imagine a specific friend who needs this information. Say the lines to them. A lens is easier to address when you picture a face behind it. Some people tape a small photo or sticky note near the lens.

### 5. Move before you speak

Stand up, shake out your shoulders, take a slow breath, and speak. A few seconds of physical reset takes some tension out. Avoid anything dramatic; the goal is just to leave your head and return to your body.

### 6. Warm up your voice

Before filming, read a paragraph aloud, hum, or say a tongue twister. Your first lines will be more natural if your voice is not cold.

## Reframe the goal

The goal is not to look flawless. It is to be **clear and present**. Viewers forgive imperfect lighting and the occasional stumble; they do not forgive boredom or confusion. Your job is to care visibly about your point.

## A session structure

A 15-minute daily practice:

1. 1 minute: physical reset, warm-up.
2. 2 minutes: record a 30-second take of anything, one pass.
3. 5 minutes: record a take of a prepared hook and payoff, three tries.
4. 2 minutes: write one observation about the takes (not judgments).
5. 5 minutes: voice note for the inbox.

## Dealing with the inner critic

* **Name it.** "That is the critic." Then return to your line.
* **Separate the roles.** The person who films is not the person who edits.
* **Compare to your own past, not others'.** Your week-1 takes are the benchmark for week 2.
* **Be kind when reviewing.** Treat the person on screen as a friend.

## Common mistakes

* **Watching every take right away.**
* **Waiting to feel ready.**
* **Doing one long session a week** instead of short daily ones.
* **Trying to copy a favorite creator's energy** instead of your own.

## Exercise

For seven days, do the 15-minute session. Keep a small log: date, what I tried, one observation. At the end of the week, watch your day-1 and day-7 takes side by side and write three things that changed. There will be some.

## Checklist

* [ ] I understand why this feels strange, and that it is normal.
* [ ] I decided not to watch takes immediately.
* [ ] I have a 15-minute daily session.
* [ ] I put a person (or a photo) behind the lens.

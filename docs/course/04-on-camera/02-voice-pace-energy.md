# Lesson 4.2: Voice, pace and energy

**English** | [Русский](../../ru/course/04-on-camera/02-voice-pace-energy.md)

**Time:** 40 minutes

## Goal

Make your speech clear and engaging on a small speaker: control pace, volume, pauses and emphasis, and match your energy to the camera.

## Why voice matters in short video

Many people watch with sound on, and the platforms reward videos with sound (Instagram lists lack of sound among the things that reduce Reels reach). In a talking video, your voice carries most of the meaning. Two creators can say the same words and one holds attention while the other loses it. The difference is usually pace, emphasis and energy, not talent.

## Energy: the camera subtracts

A camera flattens you. What feels like a lively conversation in a room often looks calm and flat on screen. A useful rule: **raise your energy about 20 percent over normal conversation**, without shouting. This does not mean acting. It means speaking as if you were explaining something you care about to a friend across a noisy cafe.

Signals of energy: varied pitch, a smile in your voice, quick but clear delivery, short pauses before important words.

## Pace

* **Speak a little faster than feels natural,** then cut the pauses in editing (lesson 6.2). Slow delivery plus long pauses reads as hesitant.
* **But vary it.** Slow down for the key phrase. Speed up for the connective tissue.
* **Aim for clarity before speed.** If a viewer must re-listen to understand, they leave.

A pace exercise: record the same 30-second script three times: at 80 percent, 100 percent and 120 percent of your natural speed. Compare. Most people find the middle or faster version works best, but yours may differ.

## Pauses and punctuation

* **Pause before the important word.** A half-second of silence tells the listener "this matters."
* **Pause at the end of a beat** so the editing can cut cleanly.
* **Avoid filler.** "Um", "like", "you know" and "so" are normal, but they dilute. Replace them with silence. Silence feels longer to the speaker than the listener.

## Emphasis

Say the sentence "I never said she stole my money" seven times, emphasizing a different word each time. The meaning changes. In your script, mark the 1-2 words in each beat that carry the meaning. Hit them.

## Volume and distance

* Speak at the volume you would use to talk to someone one meter away. Not whispering, not projecting to a room.
* Keep a steady distance from the microphone (lesson 5.4). Moving your head forward and back changes the volume.
* Don't turn your head away mid-sentence while the phone mic is on one side.

## Tone and variety

Monotone is the main enemy. To vary:

* **Pitch:** go up on questions, down on conclusions.
* **Speed:** quicken in excitement, slow for weight.
* **Volume:** drop slightly for confidences, lift for emphasis.
* **Rhythm:** alternate short and longer sentences.

Record yourself reading a children's story with exaggerated voices once. Then dial it down to 30 percent. That is a good working range.

## Breathing

Breathe before you start the sentence, not in the middle. A relaxed belly breath (inhale through the nose, low in the body) steadies the voice. If you run out of breath on a long sentence, shorten the sentence.

## Accent and language

If you speak with an accent or in a second language, that is not a problem. Clarity matters more than accent. Slow slightly on difficult words, keep sentences short, and use captions (lesson 6.3) to reinforce. Many successful creators speak in a non-native language; authenticity is an asset.

## Warm-up routine (2 minutes)

1. Roll your shoulders, relax your jaw.
2. Hum on a low note, then slide up.
3. Say a tongue twister three times ("red leather, yellow leather").
4. Read your hook aloud twice at full energy.

## Common mistakes

* **Talking at conversation energy.** On camera it looks flat.
* **Rushing without clarity.**
* **Dead air at the start.** Press record and begin; trim later.
* **Reading in a "reading voice".** Rehearse and use beats instead.

## Exercise

1. Record the same 30-second script at three speeds. Review and pick yours.
2. Record the hook with three different emotions: urgent, amused, calm. Pick the one that feels most like you.
3. Mark two emphasis words per beat on your next script.
4. Do the warm-up before your next three takes and note the difference.

## Checklist

* [ ] I raised my energy and noticed the difference.
* [ ] I found a pace that is clear and quick.
* [ ] I mark emphasis words in my beats.
* [ ] I replace fillers with silence where I can.

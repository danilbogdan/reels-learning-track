# 30-day plan

**English** | [Русский](ru/plan.md)

The lessons for each day are in the [course schedule](course/schedule.md).

No publishing for the first two weeks. The goal of weeks 1-2 is a daily filming habit and a stock of material.

| Week | Goal | Daily minimum | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | a 7-day streak | a voice note + one 30-second take | 7 raw clips |
| 2 | hooks and angles | 1 angle from the list + analysis of 1 other person's video | a table of 20 videos, 5 angles filmed |
| 3 | first posts | film 1 video a day, publish 3 over the week | 3 reels |
| 4 | review and repeat | film 1 video a day, 10 minutes on analytics | 4-5 reels, a conclusion on which hook and angle gave the best retention |

## Minimum on a bad day

Only a voice note. The point is not to break the streak, not to film a perfect shot.

## Angles for week 2

One per day:

1. Front-facing at eye level.
2. From below, looking up.
3. Over the shoulder.
4. Walking around the room while talking.
5. Close-up of hands or screen with no face.

## Table for analyzing other people's videos

Columns: link, first line, first frame, promise to the viewer, the turn, how it ended, why it works. From the table pick 3-5 recurring patterns and rework them with your own story.

## Equipment and setup before week 3

* Choose a recording format on iPhone and set up conversion to constant frame rate (see the [summary](summary.md), filming section).
* Settle on a spot and time for light: a window in front or to the side.
* Check sound: a quiet room or a lavalier mic.

## Analytics (weeks 3-4)

After each post record: hook, length, retention in the first 3 seconds, average watch time, completion rate, shares, saves. Metric names in each platform's interface may differ.

## Checkpoint after 30 reels

Count published reels, not days. When you have published 30, stop and compare before deciding what to do next. If you post a diary reel every day, this lands around day 30; at the plan's pace (about 8-9 reels in 30 days) it comes in the first block of the 90-day plan.

Put the numbers in one table, one row per reel: hook, length, retention in the first 3 seconds, average watch time, completion rate, views, shares, saves, follows.

Then answer:

* Is there a format or hook type that beats your median clearly and more than once?
* Is retention improving from the first ten reels to the last ten?
* Do views turn into follows, or only into reach?

Then pick the next step:

* **Keep going solo.** No clear signal yet. Another 30 reels with one change at a time.
* **Invest in quality.** One format works but light, sound or editing holds it back: buy or improve one element (see lessons 5.3, 5.4, 6.1).
* **Add a partner.** The format works but you lack reach or skills: try a collaboration (lesson 8.2).
* **Consider a producer or studio.** Only with a proven format and something to sell behind the account (lesson 9.1). A studio buys polish and speed; it does not create an audience.

Write the decision and one sentence of reasoning in your tracker.

## Tooling

[reel-skills](https://github.com/danilbogdan/reel-skills) is the companion toolbox. Its README describes skills for learning from reference videos (video analysis, hook linting), for making the reel (cutting pauses, B-roll indexing, rendering) and for working with notes (a notes digest). Natural fits for this plan:

* **Week 2:** analyzing other people's videos and checking your hooks.
* **Weeks 1-4:** turning voice notes into usable material.
* **Weeks 3-4:** cutting and rendering before you post.

Check the reel-skills README for current skill names and setup, since that repo evolves separately.
